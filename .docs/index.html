05. Movement Rules, Collision Semantics & MAPPO Evaluation Notes

Explicit specification of the formal rules enforced across all three algorithms and the distinction between search planning time and reinforcement learning training/inference time.

Shared 2D Problem & Collision Rules

Discrete 4-Connected Grid: Time advances in discrete steps t = 0, 1, …. At each step, an agent either waits on its current cell or moves to one of the 4 cardinal neighbors (up, down, left, right). Diagonal movement and corner-cutting are disabled across all methods.

Vertex & Edge-Swap Conflicts: Two agents may never occupy the same cell at the same timestep t (vertex conflict) and may never swap adjacent cells between t and t + 1 (edge conflict). In MAPFEnv, conflicting moves are refused and cascade to a fixed point so rollouts never violate physical occupancy.

Stay-at-Goal Occupancy: Agents remain on their goal cells after arrival. A solution is solved and valid only if every agent reaches its assigned goal at the end of the horizon and no collision occurs with parked or moving agents.

MAPPO Training vs. Evaluation Time

Architecture (Yu et al., 2022): MAPPO uses Centralized Training with Decentralized Execution (CTDE). During training, the value critic observes the normalized global state (5 × N + 1 features: all agent positions, goals, and step fraction), while the shared policy actor observes a 9×9 egocentric 4-channel window plus goal bearing/distance (327 features).

Why Training & Inference Time Are Separated: Search algorithms (Prioritized A* and CBS) compute a plan online from scratch for each problem instance. By contrast, MAPPO invests compute offline during policy training (124,928 environment steps across curriculum instances, or optional on-instance training) and executes forward neural network inference at test time. The metrics table reports Offline/Instance Training Time and Rollout Inference Time in separate rows so they are never conflated.
